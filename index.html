Data-Driven Strategy
Competitive Pricing Intelligence
Building a repeatable system to win bids and protect margins
Read Case Study ›
The Problem
In a highly competitive San Diego market, pricing too high loses jobs and pricing too low destroys margins. With ~200 inbound leads per month across Google, Yelp, phone, and walk-ins — and a 6-person sales team executing bids daily — I needed a repeatable, data-driven pricing system that protected margins without sacrificing volume.
Intelligence System
Conducted daily competitor benchmarking via Google and Yelp click volume tracking — even on days off. Mystery-shopped competitors for both materials and labor pricing. Established a pricing rule from nearly 10 years of industry knowledge: if supplier pricing was only 20–30% above cost, it signaled unfavorable manufacturer terms and triggered renegotiation or supplier replacement. This discipline drove margins from 30% to nearly 50%.
Pricing Strategy
Implemented a lump-sum pricing model to protect margins and control discount conversations. Required all itemized bids to be reviewed and approved before client delivery. Used live competitor bids shown by clients during negotiations as real-time market intelligence — turning a tense moment into a pricing opportunity. This approach drove a 90% close rate on competitive price-match situations.
Results
Maintained a 60% overall close rate across ~200 monthly leads and a 6-person sales team, with an average project value of $6,000. By identifying Yelp as the highest-ROI acquisition channel through systematic testing, reallocated $100K in annual ad spend to drive 85% of total revenue — a data-driven channel decision that directly funded business growth.
30% → 50% Margins
Grew gross margins by ~20 points through daily competitive intelligence, disciplined pricing strategy, and data-driven channel optimization across 200 monthly leads

The Challenge
Expanding into an entirely new service category required upskilling a team of 12 with zero background in plumbing, electrical, tile work, cabinetry, or full kitchen and bathroom remodeling — across 50+ new products and 10+ new services. The team had to speak confidently to customers about technically complex work, and do it fast.
Training & Enablement
Personally designed and led a hands-on training program — combining bimonthly group sessions covering product knowledge, installation mechanics, sales techniques, and role-play exercises with one-on-one live sales call shadowing. The result: the entire team was independently selling the full new product and service line within 1 month, driving a 30% increase in sales per person.
Servant Leadership in Practice
Structured all personal work around team availability — staying accessible in-store during business hours to answer questions, remove blockers, and support staff in real time. Visited job sites with lunch for installers rather than pulling them from work. Held weekly cash flow reviews with the accountant and dedicated one-on-ones with the scheduler. Organized bimonthly team dinners and game nights, plus annual holiday team builders — resulting in zero staff turnover since opening.
Operational Scale
Led a team of 12 completing 80–150 projects per month at an average value of $6,000 per project — representing up to $900K in monthly project volume. Grew the team by 2 additional employees during the expansion period to meet demand, while maintaining the culture, cohesion, and performance standards built from day one.

Product Analysis — Home Services Platform
Yelp for Business
Analyzed from the perspective of a $100K/year advertiser in the San Diego home renovation market
The Product

Yelp is a two-sided marketplace connecting consumers with local businesses through reviews, search, and advertising. It serves two distinct users: consumers seeking trusted service recommendations, and businesses competing for high-intent leads. As a flooring and remodeling company spending $100K annually on Yelp Ads, I operated deeply on both sides of this platform for years.

✓ What Works Well
  • High-intent demand generation — Yelp attracts consumers actively seeking home renovation services, making leads far warmer than social media ads.
  • Competitive intelligence — The ability to track competitor click volumes gave me real-time market data I used to adjust ad spend and pricing strategy daily.
  • Budget elasticity — Increasing ad spend produced a measurable, near-immediate increase in lead volume — a clear signal of strong supply-demand matching.
  • AI-powered quote requests — The AI intake system made customer quote requests more precise, reducing back-and-forth and improving lead quality.
  • Business tools & trust signals — License verification, review ratings, website linking, and social integration gave businesses credibility at the point of customer decision-making.
✗ What Needs Work
  • Lead list UX bug — After responding to a lead, the app resets to the top of the lead list instead of returning to the previous position — a recurring friction point that wastes time for high-volume businesses.
  • Aggressive review filtering — Legitimate 5-star reviews from real customers were regularly removed as "non-organic" despite zero solicitation — penalizing honest small businesses.
  • Forced digital tracking number — Requiring businesses to use a Yelp-generated call tracking number means customers see an unfamiliar number on callbacks, reducing answer rates and trust.
  • Hidden multi-bid feature — Customers frequently missed the option to request multiple competing bids — a core value proposition of the platform that isn't surfaced prominently enough in the consumer UX.
The Biggest Opportunity

Yelp's review filtering system is its most damaging trust problem — not for consumers, but for the businesses that fund the platform. Authentic 5-star reviews from verified customers are being removed at scale, penalizing honest businesses while bad actors find workarounds. This erodes advertiser trust and threatens long-term retention of Yelp's most valuable customers. If I were the PM, this is where I'd focus.

If I Were the PM — My Solutions
Verified User Badge

Allow consumers to privately verify their identity with Yelp while maintaining public anonymity. Verified reviewers' submissions bypass aggressive filtering — creating a trusted review tier that rewards authenticity without exposing personal data.

Geo-Verified Review Signal

Use opt-in geolocation data to confirm a reviewer visited a physical storefront. Adds a passive, low-friction authenticity signal for brick-and-mortar businesses without requiring manual verification from the customer.

Transaction-Backed Review Appeal

When a legitimate review is flagged, allow businesses to submit transaction proof — invoice, payment confirmation, or booking record — as evidence of a real customer relationship. Creates a fair, data-backed appeals process instead of a black-box removal system.

Partner-Integrated Purchase Verification

Leverage Yelp's existing partner ecosystem to automate purchase verification at scale. Bake transaction confirmation into Yelp's T&C with payment and booking partners — making authenticity signals automatic, scalable, and invisible to the end user.

$100K/Year Advertiser Perspective
This teardown is grounded in 9 years of daily platform use, $100K in annual ad spend, and firsthand experience managing hundreds of leads per month — giving it a level of operational depth that goes beyond surface-level product critique.
